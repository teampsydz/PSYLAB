ACT I · MR K

Clinical data before terminology

Each scene requires two answers: first the term, then the distinction that separates it from its most plausible neighbor. The term card unlocks only after both.

CASE 01

Distinguish decreased need for sleep from insomnia or simple voluntary sleep restriction.

CASE EXCERPT

Mr K usually sleeps 6.5–7 hours. For about 12 days he has slept roughly four hours, gets up to work, does not try to return to sleep, and reports no unusual fatigue. Normally such a short night would leave him “useless” the next day.

Which term most precisely describes the phenomenon?
🔬 Nuance checkpoint

Which piece of information is most discriminating?

CONSOLIDATED TERM

Decreased need for sleep

🧭 Less sleep + less need for sleep.

Model descriptive definition

An acquired reduction in the subjective need for sleep relative to baseline: the person sleeps substantially less and does not experience the fatigue or compensatory need for sleep that would usually follow.

Do not confuse with

Insomnia: sleep may be short in both, but with insomnia the person generally wants more sleep or feels the consequences of sleep loss.

3 acceptable descriptive formulations

  1. Decreased need for sleep described in phenomenological terms without diagnostic inference.
  2. Observable or reported change from baseline that captures the core feature of decreased need for sleep.
  3. Descriptive wording that specifies the phenomenon, its context, and its nearest boundary.

Possible wording in the clinical note

Markedly decreased need for sleep for about ten days: ~4 h/night without daytime fatigue or compensatory recovery sleep.

CASE 02

Isolate the speed of thought before interpreting its direction or coherence.

CASE EXCERPT

Mr K says, “Ideas are coming much faster. It feels like my brain is running at 200%.” At this point we do not yet know whether he loses the thread of his answers.

Which term most precisely describes the phenomenon?
🔬 Nuance checkpoint

What does this term primarily describe?

CONSOLIDATED TERM

Tachypsychia

🧭 Tachypsychia = speed of thought.

Model descriptive definition

Abnormally accelerated tempo of thinking: ideas form and succeed one another unusually quickly, subjectively or observably, without necessarily implying loss of direction or coherence.

Do not confuse with

Flight of ideas: adds rapid shifts in direction and topic to the increased speed of thought.

3 acceptable descriptive formulations

  1. Tachypsychia described in phenomenological terms without diagnostic inference.
  2. Observable or reported change from baseline that captures the core feature of tachypsychia.
  3. Descriptive wording that specifies the phenomenon, its context, and its nearest boundary.

Possible wording in the clinical note

Subjective and observable tachypsychia with accelerated succession of ideas.

CASE 03

Recognize flight of ideas and distinguish it from simple acceleration of thought.

CASE EXCERPT

While discussing a conflict with his manager, Mr K moves from the contract to organization, software, a supplier, a former colleague, and possible recruitment. The links remain understandable, but several threads are abandoned before completion. With redirection, he can return to the conflict.

Which term most precisely describes the phenomenon?
🔬 Nuance checkpoint

What adds something beyond simple tachypsychia here?

CONSOLIDATED TERM

Flight of ideas

🧭 Flight of ideas = speed + unstable direction.

Model descriptive definition

Rapid associative thinking in which thought repeatedly changes direction before the previous thread is completed, often with understandable links in milder forms.

Do not confuse with

Tachypsychia: describes speed; thought can be fast without repeatedly changing direction.

3 acceptable descriptive formulations

  1. Flight of ideas described in phenomenological terms without diagnostic inference.
  2. Observable or reported change from baseline that captures the core feature of flight of ideas.
  3. Descriptive wording that specifies the phenomenon, its context, and its nearest boundary.

Possible wording in the clinical note

Relatively organized flight of ideas: rapid associations, frequent shifts of direction, understandable links, and return to topic after redirection.

CASE 04

Distinguish a detail-laden answer from genuine loss of the original point.

CASE EXCERPT

Asked why he chose a piece of computer equipment, Mr K describes the model, specifications, seller, stock, alternatives, and delivery times. After several minutes he finally concludes that he chose it because it was immediately available.

Which term most precisely describes the phenomenon?
🔬 Nuance checkpoint

Which feature is decisive?

CONSOLIDATED TERM

Circumstantiality

🧭 Many detours, but the point is eventually reached.

Model descriptive definition

A mode of thought in which the response is burdened by excessive secondary details and detours, yet ultimately reaches the requested point or answers the original question.

Do not confuse with

Tangentiality: the response drifts to peripheral material and does not truly return to the original question.

3 acceptable descriptive formulations

  1. Circumstantiality described in phenomenological terms without diagnostic inference.
  2. Observable or reported change from baseline that captures the core feature of circumstantiality.
  3. Descriptive wording that specifies the phenomenon, its context, and its nearest boundary.

Possible wording in the clinical note

Circumstantial answer with many secondary details but eventual completion of the requested point.

CASE 05

Asked what time the argument with his manager began, Mr K talks at length about company culture, slow procedures, the current contract, and old software. Two minutes later, the time of the argument has still not been given.

Tangentiality

🧭 Around the point, never to the point.

Model descriptive definition

  3. Descriptive wording that specifies the phenomenon, its context, and its nearest boundary.

Possible wording in the clinical note

Tangential response: drift toward related themes without directly answering the original question.

CASE 08

Distractibility
